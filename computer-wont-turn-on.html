Computer Won't Turn On? Safe Checks to Try

If your desktop PC or laptop appears dead, do a few simple checks before buying parts or reinstalling Windows. The first goal is to identify whether the problem is power, display, or Windows startup.

Safety first

Stop if you smell burning, see smoke, hear unusual electrical noises, or notice a swollen laptop battery. Disconnect power if safe and seek qualified repair. Do not open a power supply or a battery pack.

1. Check what “won't turn on” means

  • No lights or fan: the device may not be receiving power.
  • Lights or fans, but no picture: check the display, monitor input and cable.
  • Manufacturer logo appears, then it stops: the computer may be failing during startup.
  • “No boot device” or similar message: the system may not be finding a bootable drive.

2. Try these basic power checks

  1. Check the wall socket with a device you know works, if safe.
  2. For a desktop, make sure the power cable is firmly connected at the wall and computer. If the power supply has a rear switch, check that it is on; do not open the case.
  3. For a laptop, use the correct compatible charger and inspect the cable for visible damage. Do not use a damaged charger.
  4. Disconnect non-essential USB devices, docks and external drives, then try starting once more.
  5. If a desktop powers on but the screen is blank, check that the monitor is on and set to the correct input. Reseat external display cables only while devices are powered off.

3. If it turns on but Windows does not start

Write down the exact error message and note whether it began after an update, power outage or hardware change. If Windows opens, back up important files before attempting major repairs. Avoid changing BIOS/UEFI settings randomly, formatting drives, or reinstalling Windows before checking for data loss risk.

  • For Windows startup and repair issues, see our Windows PC troubleshooting guide.
  • For portable device symptoms, see our laptop repair guide.

4. When to stop troubleshooting

Seek qualified help if the device repeatedly shuts down, overheats, has liquid damage, makes grinding or clicking noises, cannot detect its storage drive, or contains important files that are not backed up. Hardware diagnosis may require physical inspection.

Need help choosing the next step?

Describe the device, lights/fan behaviour and the exact error message. Do not send passwords, OTPs or private documents.
